### 1. **Does ethics provide a list of "right answers"?**
   - No, moral philosophy or ethics explains what one ought to do.

### 2. **How can working with people of different backgrounds help when considering ethical questions?**
   - It helps us understand moral relativism, the fact that there may not be a universally good answer for all.

### 3. **What was the role of IBM in Nazi Germany? Why did the company participate as it did? Why did the workers participate?**
   - By turning a Nelsonian eye, IBM supplied the Nazis with data tabulation products necessary to track the extermination of Jews and other groups on a massive scale. 
   - This was driven from the top, with marketing directly to Hitler and his leadership team.
   - Project managers, engineers, and technicians were just living ordinary lives, following orders, and pursuing business goals.
   - The destruction of the Jewish people became secondary due to IBM's technical achievements and the fantastical profits during global economic hardship.

### 4. **What was the role of the first person jailed in the Volkswagen diesel scandal?**
   - The first jailed person was James Liang, an engineer who implemented the cheating software. He followed orders, while higher-ups avoided direct accountability.

### 5. **What was the problem with a database of suspected gang members maintained by California law enforcement officials?**
   - The database was full of errors, including 42 babies listed as gang members under the age of one.
   - There was no process for correcting mistakes or removing people once added, undermining data ethics and recourse.

### 6. **Why did YouTube's recommendation algorithm recommend videos of partially clothed children to pedophiles?**
   - The algorithm recommended videos based on users’ viewing patterns, promoting unwatched videos classified as similar, creating harmful feedback loops unintentionally.

### 7. **What are the problems with the centrality of metrics?**
   - The relentless optimization of a single metric can create unintended consequences, such as exploitable edge cases and feedback loops.

### 8. **Why did Meetup.com not include gender in its recommendation system for tech meetups?**
   - Male-dominated interest skewed recommendations, reducing visibility for females. Removing the gender parameter helped mitigate this bias.

### 9. **What are the six types of bias in machine learning, according to Suresh and Guttag?**
   - _Historical Bias:_ Bias stemming from systemic inequities or societal patterns reflected in the training data.
   - _Representation Bias:_ Bias caused by underrepresentation or overrepresentation of certain groups or features in the dataset.
   - _Measurement Bias:_ Bias introduced when data collection or labeling methods systematically distort reality.
   - _Aggregation Bias:_ Bias arising from assuming homogeneity in diverse populations, ignoring subgroup differences.
   - _Evaluation Bias:_ Bias occurring when evaluation metrics disproportionately favor certain groups or contexts.
   - _Deployment Bias:_ Bias introduced when a model is applied in a context different from its training environment.

   - Prejudice in the judicial system, an all white jury more likey to convict a black defendent 

### 11. **Where are most images in ImageNet from?**
  - The vast majority of the images are from the United States and other Western countries, leading to models trained on ImageNet performing worse on scenes from other countries and cultures.

### 12. **In the paper ["Does Machine Learning Automate Moral Hazard and Error"](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/sendhil/files/aer.p20171084.pdf) why is sinusitis found to be predictive of a stroke?**
   - Sinusitis is predictive of stroke in the model because it is a proxy for healthcare-seeking behavior, which depends on factors like socioeconomic status, access to healthcare, and systemic inequalities. 
   - This highlights how measurement bias can lead models to prioritise variables unrelated to the actual medical condition they aim to predict.

### 13. **What is representation bias?**
   - Representation bias occurs when imbalances in the training data, such as over- or underrepresentation of certain groups, lead models to amplify these disparities, as seen when gender imbalances in occupations are exaggerated by predictions.

### 14. **How are machines and people different, in terms of their use for making decisions?**
   - Machine learning can create feedback loops: Small amounts of bias can rapidly increase exponentially due to feedback loops.
   - Machine learning can amplify bias: Human bias can lead to larger amounts of machine learning bias.
   - Algorithms & humans are used differently: Human decision makers and algorithmic decision makers are not used in a plug-and-play interchangeable way in practice.
   - Technology is power: And with that comes responsibility.

### 15. **Is disinformation the same as "fake news"?**
   - Disinformation differs from false information or fake news.
   - Disinformation often contain seeds of truth, or half-truths taken out of context.
   - In The KGB and Soviet Disinformation (Pergamon) he wrote, "Most campaigns are a carefully designed mixture of facts, half-truths, exaggerations, and deliberate lies."

### 16. **Why is disinformation through auto-generated text a particularly significant issue?**
   - Disinformation through autogenerated text is a particularly significant issue, due to the greatly increased capability provided by deep learning.

### 17. **What are the five ethical lenses described by the Markkula Center?**
   - The rights approach: Which option best respects the rights of all who have a stake?
   - The justice approach: Which option treats people equally or proportionately?
   - The utilitarian approach: Which option will produce the most good and do the least harm?- The common good approach: Which option best serves the community as a whole, not just some members?
   - The virtue approach: Which option leads me to act as the sort of person I want to be?

### 18. **Where is policy an appropriate tool for addressing data ethics issues?**
   - Policy is essential for addressing systemic failures, misaligned profit incentives, and societal harms like biased algorithms and privacy violations.
   - It provides coordinated regulation and accountability where individual actions or technical fixes fall short.
